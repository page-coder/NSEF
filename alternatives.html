Policy alternatives and trade-offs

1. Improve existing arrangements

Focus on better implementation, clearer information, reduced application barriers, stronger monitoring, and better targeting within present schemes. Benefits may include lower disruption; risks include leaving structural gaps unresolved. Evaluation needs administrative and access data.

2. Add situation-based support

Consider additional needs-based scholarships, educational assistance, outreach, or complementary welfare while preserving existing protections. This could address overlooked hardship; it also requires clear rules, data quality, legal review, and safeguards against exclusion.

3. Consider extensive restructuring

This is a high-complexity option requiring substantial empirical research, constitutional analysis, broad consultation, and close examination of unintended effects on different groups. It should not be presumed preferable simply because it is more extensive.
