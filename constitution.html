Constitution & law

This is an educational overview, not legal advice. Constitutional text, court decisions, administrative rules, and NSEF’s proposal are distinct.

Relevant constitutional provisions

  • Article 14: equality before law and equal protection of laws.
  • Article 15: prohibits specified discrimination; clauses 15(4), 15(5), and 15(6) provide for particular special provisions, including EWS in clause 15(6).
  • Article 16: equality of opportunity in public employment; clauses 16(4) and 16(6) concern particular provisions including EWS.
  • Article 46: directs the State to promote educational and economic interests of weaker sections, particularly SCs and STs.
  • Article 368: sets the constitutional amendment procedure.

Primary text: Constitution of India, India Code. The precise operation of each provision depends on text, case law, and context.

Key judgments

Indra Sawhney v. Union of India (1992) is a major decision on reservations under Article 16(4). Janhit Abhiyan v. Union of India (2022) considered the 103rd Constitutional Amendment concerning EWS provisions. Neither case can be reduced to a general authorization to abolish or restructure all existing categories; legal consequences depend on the specific measure.

Primary judgments: Supreme Court of India judgments portal. Readers should consult the full judgments and qualified legal analysis for contested questions.

NSEF’s legal limit

NSEF does not declare extensive restructuring constitutionally valid. Any proposal would require programme-specific constitutional analysis, legislative competence review, and scrutiny of effects and safeguards.
