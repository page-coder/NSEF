National Social Equity Framework · India

Equal Opportunity Requires Understanding Different Forms of Disadvantage.

NSEF is an independent research proposal examining how economic hardship, educational barriers, social disadvantage, and unequal access to opportunity can be considered in public policy while respecting constitutional protections and the need to address discrimination.

Explore the frameworkRead the research
Independent proposal. NSEF is not a Government of India initiative, a government department, an approved reservation reform, or a political party platform.

The policy question

Support should respond to real barriers—without erasing different histories.

People can face serious barriers because of household poverty, poor school access, disability, geography, weak information networks, or discrimination. These barriers do not always operate in the same way, and a fair response may require more than one tool.

NSEF asks how public programmes can identify unmet needs while preserving constitutional protections that address caste-based exclusion and representation.

A framework under examination

1. Understand circumstances

Examine economic, educational, geographic, disability-related, and social barriers with care. Caste identity alone does not determine a person’s household circumstances.

2. Keep distinctions clear

Household poverty and caste discrimination can overlap, but they are not interchangeable. Existing protections require serious analysis.

3. Test before scaling

Any new approach needs reliable data, legal review, public consultation, pilots, and independent evaluation—not assumptions.
